Something that has always filled me with a very unique sense of joy, is contemplating the miracle of living. It doesn't take much thought. It can be as simple as: there either is, or there either isn't. This might seem obvious, in fact, it is, but when you think only of that, and put aside everything inside of you that has been conditioned to ignore such obvious things, it really is amazing. Being alive. This reality. That there is anything at all. And not only are we alive but we are conscious animals. How about that? And with thought comes logic and abstraction, so much more impossible depth to what is, and even what isn't! Creativity, the arts!

It really is something, being me. Not that my life is in any way interesting. It would be less humble of me and rather more of a complaint to say that my life on the surface is rather dull. But being me, you see, from my perspective, this is all I have. Everything that is not me is external. And I cannot read minds to prove intention, nor can I truly ascertain in any meaningful way the veracity of another's existence without this critical piece of evidence. Why then? Why should it be me? Here and now. Of all times past and future. Of all places. To be alive. Me. And not many things or people, or nothing at all. Not a plant, not a bird, not a tree. I am a composition of elements brought to, what we like to call: life. It's difficult. It really is. To accept that fact. Even harder to feel satisfied in what appreciation you can show for having life. It's strange. Thinking about this. It's like looking in a mirror, but in a different way altogether. I feel that people who take photos of themselves quite often might have a certain numbness to their appearance. It's that familiarity, you see. As opposed to someone who many only look in the mirror once a day, or maybe not even at all! Imagine never having seen what you look like; who, or what you are. Not having an idea of what your face looks like at all. Can you picture that? For me, thinking about this sort of thing, it feels like that. Like looking in a mirror in the dark, seeing only an outline of a person, and still never having seen my face.

I wish I had better words to convey this strange feeling to you. Then again, maybe it's for the best. Nothing too good has come from my unfocused existentialist thinking. But this. Existence. Being me in particular. That my life had a beginning, and it will have an end. And by atheist thought, there is nothing after. I was not promised a life. Whatever I was before I was me, if I was anything at all, I didn't have a life. Eventually the part of me that lives will cease forever. Going to sleep for the last time never to wake again. How terrifying. But... how nice it's been to have anything at all.
