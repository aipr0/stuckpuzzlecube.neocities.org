Why I really felt like talking about Fallout 3 is because a part of the main quest brings you to simulated reality. On the quest to find your father you track him down to another vault. So you find him in a "tranquility lounger", and you find that you have to step inside too. Immediately you notice this loud and cheerful tune playing, and everything is in black and white. Suddenly you're in pre-war America, and everything is fine and dandy. You speak with everyone, knowing they're all hooked into a simulation just as you are, but they don't have a clue. You speak with a young girl named Betty, quite literally at the centre of this tiny little world surrounded by suburban houses facing inwards. You get the idea that maybe she isn't really just a little girl. She wants to play a game. If you oblige, you wind up massacring everyone in the end, only for them to be brought back to life with no memory of what just happened. If you're smart, you can find access to controls that will enable a Chinese communist invasion protocol, massacring everyone, but this time for good, ending the cycle of torment. Enacting out the simulated murders nets you negative karma. Actually killing everyone rewards you good karma. I thought that was interesting. Anyway Betty turns out to be Braun who is actually some evil German genius who has been running torment nexus simulations for hundreds of years. They're powerless to do anything so they answer all your questions, and shoo you away admittedly quite gracefully considering the situation and also the fact that they will be stuck forever alone in the world they've created for themselves.

This part of the game was always a memorable moment for me. May be one of the few memorable moments from the game at all. But anyway, it always stood out, just because well, it does stick out, like a thousand sore thumbs. The lack of colour, the music, the peacefulness, the absurdity. But none of the... underlying nature of that world really ever stuck with me. Simulated reality. Maybe it's because it was such a boring little world. Though, it was enough to satisfy this one monster in particular. And it makes me think. I have myself quite a number of boring little fantasies. But they're mine. I'm always worrying about dying suddenly today, and wishing on living forever. Both thoughts terrify me equally. So bored that you could die. That you would *want* to die.

But of course, if someone has cured death, then surely they must have cured boredom too. I am not being facetious or flippant.
