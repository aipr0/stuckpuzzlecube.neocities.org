A worrying title for a blog post. No, it won't be that interesting.

To start this off with something typical. Games. I played the Dead Space remake not too long ago. Probably couldn't have played it in a much more worse time in my life. Anyway, it was phenomenal. Lots of fun. Very satisfying. Even towards the end when you're outfitted like a tank, it still scares the shit out of me. I like that over the course of the game, at least to me, it seems to evolve from survival horror to... well, it's still of the horror genre. Horror action? I then played Cyberpunk 2077. I have been putting this off for a while. I considered modding it, then I considered against it, because it would only further delay me playing it and I don't have a good baseline for what I want to change or add to the game. Apart from the demo they released last decade, I went into it pretty much blind. I wasn't impressed. Yeah! It's a few years old now, but I guess Twitter lied to me about the improvements. The world is nowhere as alive as I wanted it to be, not even close. You'd think in a city where the news is essentially Cocomelon for adults, quite literally in the startup intro video informing you some fifty-odd people have been murdered, you'd think that everyone's packing. No. You punch a wall and everyone starts gayly fast-walking away on their tip-toes in single file with their hands at their sides. The game suffers for being in an open world, and has no fantasy charm to fill in the swaths of empty space between locations. I need to play it a lot more to give a more informed opinion, but I guess I'm tired of the "twelve hours to get to fun part" type game. Which leads me to the next game.

I haven't been socialising much. The only people that I really care about are online, and since having gotten rid of Discord, I've been much more to myself. Still. I text friends. I text my best friend Adam, and we talk about, well, whatever. In the usual format we always do. About five topics to reply to per spread of messages. He mentioned playing the new Control sequel, and we talked about Control as a franchise, and I told him I didn't believe for a moment it was a horror game at all, because he absolutely cannot stand playing horror games. You should see him play Phasmophobia. Joy! Anyway. I gave Control a go. Again, I'm not really impressed. One of those games you need to play through a third of before enjoying it. The last Remedy game I played was Quantum Break. I had put that off for so long and I would like to think I really gave that an honest shot. I can't remember what my complaints were at the time specfically. I guess it just felt artificial and forced. And I remember, really, really not liking the dialogue. I found it so irritating that I turned the dialogue volume off. And the gameplay was eh. But back to Control, you know what? They made your player character about as fast and acrobatic in movement, as much as you can before calling it freakish. It feels good to play! And then the fun parts are broken up with absolute babble. I guess it's supposed to be a confusing game. But what are the themes? To me it just seems like it rips off too much from X-Files and Twin Peaks, and the entire setting is retro styled federal government office. Oh, and can't forget the squares. You know, like those Warframe mastery levels? Empty white space, platforming on boxes and greebled boxy whatever. Cyberpunk actually does this with a virtual reality setting for some combat tutorials. It's that sort of thing. It's visually impressive seeing this corrupted office that's gone all blocky and choppy slot back into place but, boy howdy. They really let Sam Lake off the chain huh? I thought I used to like Alan Wake because I played it on the Xbox as a pre-teen. I still do, because there is some charm there, even if a lot of it is borrowed from well, again, Twin Peaks. It is precisely the same setting. Alan Wake I think does a good enough job of introducing you to the main character and his motivation or his struggle. Control throws you into it, which I like, but you don't know anything. Things just kind of happen. An interesting idea if done right but I just don't think the creative team behind it really gets intrigue well enough. I don't care about the main character. Oh, and you, the player character are involved as voice inside the protagonist's head. Just like Zach from Deadly Premonition! Pretty much exactly like Zach. Oh and the main character has a brother that was kidnapped from the uh Control bureau sixteen or seventeen years ago. I DON'T CARE YOU DIDN'T DO ANYTHING TO MAKE ME CARE ABOUT ANY OF THAT. So away I am shooting at zombified government employees. And the gameplay is really good but I just don't care. Oh, the world building is done with pickup readables? Okay well there's like five or more per area and it does nothing for me. The pacing just isn't great. The characters are fairly wooden, and are tracing that line of Forspoken like dialogue. The main character just is not unique at all. She's a bit of a looker, sure. She's a fish out of water character with an imaginary friend that doesn't just seem to quickly adjust to the situation they're in, they're just fine with it. Back to Dead Space. You fucking stomp this thing that just jumped out of a vent and grabbed you and the poor guy is fucking screaming, wheezing, you can hear his heart thumping, his heavy breathing, and it continues a while after the whole affair. And the more you stomp or melee the more intense it all gets. It's such a beautifully blended curve of intensity. In Control, I'll paraphrase a run of dialogue.

Second NPC you meet: "You're the director now!"
PC, to herself: "Should I tell her that it looked like the director killed himself?"
PC: "The director killed himself."
Second NPC you meet: "Oh, okay."
PC, leaving, to herself: "I'll show them. I'm not the director. I'm not anybody's director."

It's... not as bad as that. But what the fuck is that dialogue?

When I was sorting my shit out a few weeks ago, at least I thought I was before I started spiralling again, I was playing Fallout Shelter on my Steam Deck as a way to unwind between bursts of work. I kept playing it. And it kept consuming my time. Fun! But so incredibly mindless and not at all fulfilling in anyway. I've played more than enough of it. You can keep playing it and playing it, and to get all your guys all levelled up and geared out. When I first played it on my phone I quit after Deathclaws decimated my vault. I got past that point. I've done some quests. I got a lot of junk (yes, literally junk). This is why I'm making this post, or why I've titled it that. You spend enough time on something and you don't want to let it go. But I really am wasting my life playing that game. It actually occurs to me I have put off so many more games that I really, truly enjoy, because I worry about wasting my time with them. And then I wind up playing something like this because of that. Jesus Christ. I think so much of what I do or so much of how I act is twisted in that awful way. Doing X will take up too much time, so I'll just do a little bit of Y instead. And then it's all just Y. Why indeed.

There's more I need to let go of. And it's difficult. Because so much of what I need to let go of is so much a part of me. I really would like to backup all of my Twitter likes and bookmarks and delete my account. How difficult they have made it for us to do so. I have partially succeeded in batch downloading a small portion of my saved media. But with so much in-between that for some reason hasn't downloaded. So, what am I to do with an account I no longer want, but a plethora of digital joy I want to keep? Normal people would just keep the account. And it's frustrating too because for all the things I've saved, it's not worth trying to search for any of it. In fact, for some reason, the search function only seems to go back as far as a few months AT MOST. So what the fuck is the point of that? Is the philosophy behind it really just supposed to be immediate interaction with posts, and maybe to dig through a few days back for something to bring back up again? There is a point I fear that I will need to let go. Just delete the account. I can delete the account, and I can reinstall it. Deleting an account that I've near perfectly curated to actually be a feed of exactly what I want however... well, that will be a lot, lot more effort to recover.

Whining about games and being on the internet again. I've started regular exercise again. Gives me an awful head rush so I can hardly push myself the way I like. Honestly, the bad thoughts just creep in and I think "maybe this is it?". I'm getting bored of it. My nervous system isn't. But I am. This has been an entirely new challenge for me. One I'm not faring all too well with. I can fight the bad thoughts and come out on top. But against physical sensation, it is more difficult than to hope and wish the feelings away. 

I unboxed my Granddad's new air fryer. His birthday party is on the 17th. Yeah, a new one. I doubt he'll use this one much but what else do I get him?

I start work again on Friday. This wretched cycle goes on. 

There have been a few much more interesting topics I wanted to talk about, but they've fallen out of mind. And now my head has begun to hurt. Pressure in the left temple. God. Will it end? I'm going to stop writing now.

So much more I wanted out of life. Will I live to see it? And if there is an afterlife, will I have the courage to let go of my earthly desires? Truly what is at the core of my soul?
